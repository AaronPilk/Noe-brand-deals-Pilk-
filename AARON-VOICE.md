# Aaron's Writing Voice — Style Guide

How Aaron writes brand-deal emails as Talent Manager for Noe Varner. Every rule below is grounded in a verbatim quote from his real sent mail (voice-raw.md, Jul 21–30 2026). Use this to draft a reply that sounds like him — or to check one that's supposed to.

**Signs as:** "Aaron" alone on short replies; "— Aaron / Talent Manager, Noe Varner" or "Best, / Aaron / Talent Manager for Noe Varner" on templates. Real name on account: Aaron Pilkington.

---

## The one-line version

Sharp operator, not a hype man. Opens with a quick genuine acknowledgment, states the number as a fact, asks for one thing back, closes on a bare "Thanks!". No emojis, no corporate filler, no fake urgency. Keeps em-dashes.

---

## Greetings — which one, when

He picks the greeting by deal temperature. It's a tell.

- **"Hey [First]," — casual, warm, fast.** Default for quick replies, brokers, booking a call, friendly declines.
  > "Hey David," (booking a call) · "Hey Arthur," (broker) · "Hey Philip," (soft decline) · "Hey Sidhant," (counter)
- **"Hi [First]," — slightly more formal.** First contact, agencies, when he's establishing authority.
  > "Hi Clara," · "Hi Vinod," · "Hi Brandon," · "Hi [Name]," (the "three things" template)
- **"Hello [First]," — the money-moment register.** Shows up on nearly every acceptance, counter, or floor-hold. When numbers are live, he moves to "Hello."
  > "Hello Rachel," · "Hello Olga," · "Hello Palak," · "Hello Soren," · "Hello Vicky," · "Hello Alan," · "Hello Mini_印尼,"
- **Mirrors the counterpart's name formatting exactly**, including foreign scripts: "Hello Mini_印尼,".
- **Doesn't fix small typos in the greeting** when moving fast: "Hellow Villela,".

Rule of thumb: **Hey = casual/quick · Hi = first-contact/formal · Hello = a number is on the table.**

## Sign-offs

- **Bare "Thanks!" on its own line** is his signature closer on short negotiation replies. Also "Thank you!" on declines.
  > "Thanks!" (Samples 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 18) · "Thank you!" (Sample 15)
- **Template / first-contact close:** "— Aaron / Talent Manager, Noe Varner" (em-dash) or "Best, / Aaron / Talent Manager for Noe Varner".
- **Two title variants, interchangeable:** "Talent Manager, Noe Varner" and "Talent Manager for Noe Varner".
- **Warm-close one-liners:** "Looking forward to meeting!" · "Looking forward to hearing from you." · "Do not hesitate to reach out to us anytime!" · "feel free to reach out anytime!"

## Sentence rhythm & length

- **Short.** Most negotiation replies are **2–4 sentences.** Gets to the number fast, asks one question, signs "Thanks!".
- **One idea per paragraph, blank line between.** Even the long emails (templates, the Viral Mind counter, the Arcads concepts) are tightly blocked — no walls of text.
- **Opens with a single-line acknowledgment, then pivots to business.** He never skips it and never over-does it.
  > "Appreciate you reaching out about [Brand]." · "Appreciate the detailed breakdown, and FlexAura's growth is genuinely impressive." · "Great to hear from you!"
- **One move per email, one question per email.** Replies almost always end on a question or a clear next step — never a dead end.
  > "Is this something you would be interested in?" · "What are you looking for?" · "One quick question, will you running ads on this video?"

## Punctuation habits

- **Keeps em-dashes. Uses them freely.** This is real and load-bearing — do not strip them. (See "Discrepancy to resolve" below.)
  > "we don't do commission-only or CPM-only deals — full stop" · "Noe's audience is 110K+ followers and 600+ paying community members — almost all business owners…" · "— Aaron" as a sign-off
- **Loves the exclamation "Thanks!" / "Thank you!"** — but doesn't stack exclamation points elsewhere.
- **Rhetorical colon to tee up a move:** "Let's make this easy: let me know your maximum offer."
- **Fast replies run a little rough — he doesn't sanitize them.** Occasional run-ons, comma-where-a-period-goes, missing spaces:
  > "our starting price is $2500 per post we keep it very exclusive" · "if this works let me know and we can get things moving," · "the other details.I look forward"

Don't over-polish a draft trying to sound like him. A little friction reads as human.

## Capitalization & numbers

- **Standard sentence case.** No ALL-CAPS for emphasis — he **bolds** instead ("$4,000 base").
- **Dollar amounts are inconsistent by speed:** "$2500" (no comma) in fast replies, "$2,500" (comma) in templates/formal quotes. Leans no-comma when typing quickly.
- Brand names, "Noe," "Meta" always capitalized correctly.

---

## Contractions & register

- **Uses contractions naturally** — "don't," "we're," "let's," "here's," "that's." He's casual, not stiff.
- **Runs casual-professional.** Friendly and direct, never buttoned-up corporate, never salesy-hype.
- Warmth without hype: "Great to hear from you!" · "glad we're past all that."

---

## Emoji policy

- **Zero. Never.** Aaron uses no emojis in any sample. Counterparties send ✨😊💰 — he never mirrors them. Don't add any.

## Words & phrases he actually uses (quote bank)

Pull from these — they're his real language:

- "Aaron here. I manage Noe's brand partnerships, so pricing and scheduling run through me."
- "Quick context on how we work, because it's different from most creators you'll talk to:"
- "One quick question:" (his pivot to attach a scope or diligence ask)
- "Let's make this easy:" / "To make things easier"
- "That's a small discount for you and is more aligned with how we promote brands."
- "full stop"
- "in Noe's native style" / "native to how Noe talks to business owners"
- "if your product genuinely fits what Noe would use himself"
- "protect that trust" / "audience trust"
- "get this rolling" / "get things moving" / "lock the content calendar" / "start filming"
- "110K+ followers and 600+ paying community members — almost all business owners actively buying AI tools" (his standard audience stat)

## Words & phrases he NEVER uses

- **No corporate throat-clearing:** "I hope this email finds you well," "circling back," "per my last email," "synergy," "leverage," "unlock," "in today's landscape."
- **No hype adjectives about Noe** ("amazing," "incredible reach") — he states numbers instead.
- **No groveling, no over-apologizing.** Declines are one line.
- **No padding.** One opening acknowledgment, then business.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Open with one genuine acknowledgment, then pivot | Stack pleasantries or explain the obvious |
| Keep it 2–4 sentences on live negotiations | Write walls of text |
| State the number as a fact | Hedge or ask permission to quote |
| End on a question or clear next step | Leave a dead-end reply |
| Close with bare "Thanks!" | Sign "Best regards," / "Warm wishes," |
| Keep em-dashes | Strip dashes or swap them for "x" |
| Use "Hello" when a number's on the table | Use the same greeting for every email |
| Bold for emphasis | ALL-CAPS or exclamation-stacking |
| Let fast replies run slightly rough | Over-polish into corporate-clean |
| Use zero emojis | Mirror the counterpart's emojis |
| Say no warmly, one line, door open | Grovel or burn the bridge |
| Trade any discount for more deliverables | Just drop the price |

## Worked examples (situation → how Aaron writes it)

**1. Booking a call (prospect wants to talk)**
> Hey David,
>
> Yes, let's book a call. Here is our calendar link. https://cal.com/noe-varner/partnership-call
>
> Looking forward to meeting!

*Why it's him: "Hey" for a warm quick reply, one line of business, warm-close one-liner, no signature block.*

**2. Accepting a workable cap while upselling volume**
> Hello Soren,
>
> Since this is our first time collaborating I am willing to accept the $1,200 price. One quick question: can we make it 3 videos at $3,000? That's a small discount for you and is more aligned with how we promote brands.
>
> Let me know what you think.
>
> Thanks!

*Why it's him: "Hello" (money moment), a named reason for the concession, "One quick question:" pivot to expand scope, frames the discount as a favor to them, bare "Thanks!".*

**3. Graceful decline, door left open**
> Hey Philip,
>
> I am sorry we can not accept that. As you grow please feel free to contact us again!
>
> Thank you!

*Why it's him: one line, warm, no groveling, invitation to come back, "Thank you!".*

**4. Firm "no" to a commission-only structure**
> Hey Sidhant,
>
> Appreciate the detailed breakdown, and FlexAura's growth is genuinely impressive.
>
> That said, we don't do commission-only or CPM-only deals — full stop. Noe's content moves product for the brands he works with, and guaranteed base pay is how we protect the time and audience trust that makes that true.

*Why it's him: genuine acknowledgment first, then a hard line with "full stop," explains WHY on principle, keeps the em-dash, "audience trust" value language.*

## Discrepancy to resolve — em-dashes

**The real emails keep em-dashes. The CRM draft-generator strips them.** These conflict.

- **What his actual sent mail shows:** Aaron uses em-dashes naturally and often — in body copy, in his audience stat, and in his "— Aaron" sign-off (see quotes above).
- **What the CRM enforces:** `scripts/build-data.mjs` was built to a "No dashes of any kind" rule (comment, line 135). It replaces em/en-dashes in subjects with " x " (line 213: `dr.subject.replace(/\s*[—–]\s*/g, ' x ')`) and **hard-fails the build if any dash appears in a draft body** (line 219: `if (/[—–]/.test(dr.body) ...) process.exit(1)`).
- **The conflict:** the generator produces dash-free drafts that don't match Aaron's real voice, which keeps dashes.
- **For Aaron to decide:** which is canonical — the real-voice em-dash, or the CRM's no-dash rule? This doc documents the real emails as-is (dashes kept). **No code was changed.** If the real voice wins, the dash-stripping in `build-data.mjs` should be revisited.
